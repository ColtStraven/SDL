### Observations

These patterns are new and **not yet explained**. Treat them as leads for Milestone 3, not as findings.

1. **`aircraft_small` runs at a steady 4 Hz** (VERIFIED for this file). There are exactly 240 per full minute. The interval between packets has a median of 250 ms, a 10th–90th percentile range of 248–252 ms, and a maximum of 620 ms. Every packet has a distinct timestamp.
2. **`aircraft_small` size steps up in plateaus** (DERIVED). The size holds constant for long stretches, then jumps:

   | From (s after first timestamp) | Payload size (bytes) |
   |---|---|
   | 0 | 19 |
   | ~40 | 65 |
   | ~100 | 67 |
   | ~110 | 1,101 |
   | ~120 | 1,693 |
   | ~150 | 1,819 |
   | by 240 | 2,717 |
   | ~540 | 2,814–2,820 |
   | last 23 packets | 2,653 |

   A size that holds steady for minutes points to a structure made of fixed-size blocks whose number changes (for example, units being tracked), not one aircraft state per packet. EXPERIMENTAL; needs the payload decoder.
3. **This replay contains the first `aircraft_small` packets under 36 bytes.** The 19-byte packets cover roughly the first 40 s, before the size steps up. They are the smallest examples seen so far and a good starting point for reverse-engineering the layout.
4. **The `mpi` rate climbs from ~2,300 to ~23,000 per minute** during the first 3 minutes, then holds steady. This likely tracks battle activity, but it has not been decoded.
5. **ECS bursts.** There are large ECS payloads at 9.5–10.6 s (27,279, 49,199 and 69,679 bytes) and at 68.8–69.1 s (27,279, 77,871 and 208,943 bytes). There is also a burst of 1,142 ECS packets between 520 and 530 s after the first timestamp. The cause is unknown.
6. **`player_count` = 2 and `time_limit` = 30.** These are REPORTED header values. Their exact meaning (whether players means humans or slots, and whether the limit is in minutes) is not established.

## 14. Flight and weapon data (decoder `flight` command)

Produced by `sdl-replay-decoder flight`, which writes `flight_summary.json`, `ownship.jsonl`, `cockpit.jsonl` and `weapons.jsonl`. Layouts and evidence are documented in [FORMAT_FINDINGS §4](../static57-sdl/sdl-replay-decoder/docs/FORMAT_FINDINGS.md).

### 14.1 How it was decoded

- **Cockpit instruments** come from MPI message `0xD04A ReplayCockpitParams`. The layout is byte-aligned: a declared length, then pairs of id and value. All 2,495 messages parse with the declared length matching exactly (VERIFIED).
- **Author position** comes from the leading record of each `aircraft_small` sync. Packed payloads are read most-significant-bit first, with multi-byte values assembled little-endian. That convention was found by searching for the altitude value from the cockpit instruments.
- **Position check:** every position's height is compared with the cockpit altimeter at the nearest time. A sample is VERIFIED when they agree within 20 m. **All 2,494 agree**: median difference 0.45 m, maximum 11.3 m.
- **Second copy:** a second copy of the position at the end of each sync agrees within 1 cm in 2,494 of 2,494 packets.
- **x and z** are now independently checked against localhost:8111 (section 15): RMS 2.2 m over 113 map positions. The track is also continuous: no speed jumps, a maximum derived speed of 214 m/s against a maximum IAS of 204 m/s, and a median ground-speed/IAS ratio of 1.076.
- **Instrument consistency:** vertical speed predicted from airspeed and pitch (`−airspeed·sin(pitch)`) correlates with the reported vertical speed at r = 0.982.
